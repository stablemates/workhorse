---
title: Should you use Workhorse?
description: Where a PostgreSQL-native queue beats a dedicated broker, where it loses, and which tool to reach for instead.
---

Picking a queue is mostly picking how many systems you are willing to operate. A dedicated broker
scales on its own hardware and fails on its own schedule. A database-backed queue shares both with
the data it protects. This page covers what Workhorse trades away, what it buys, and the cases
where another tool is the better answer.

## The trade

Workhorse puts your jobs in the same PostgreSQL instance as your business rows. That single choice
produces every advantage and every limit below.

**You get:** an enqueue that commits with your data, one backup that covers rows and jobs together,
one set of credentials, and one place to query when something looks wrong.

**You pay:** queue throughput now competes with application queries for the same connections, the
same CPU, and the same write-ahead log. Scaling the queue means scaling the database.

## When Workhorse fits

- **Your application already runs on PostgreSQL.** Adding a queue costs a schema install, not a
  service.
- **A job must not exist unless its row does.** Transactional enqueue removes the outbox table and
  the relay process that usually carry this.
- **You need to answer what happened.** Attempt history, timelines, and dead letters are rows you
  can join against your own data.
- **Your team is small.** One database to back up, monitor, and page for.

## When to reach for something else

- **The queue must scale independently of the database.** A dedicated broker is the right tool.
  Sustained high-rate work will hit database limits long before it hits queue logic.
- **You need a workflow definition language.** Workhorse gives durable primitives — checkpoints,
  signals, waits, child jobs — but orchestration stays application code. Temporal owns that job.
- **Your work is not transactional.** If nothing is written alongside the job, the main advantage
  does not apply, and a broker you already run is simpler.
- **You are not on PostgreSQL 15 or newer.** There is no supported path below that line.

## Against the near neighbours

This table compares operational shape, not feature counts. Two questions decide most of it: **can a
job commit with your data**, and **what else do you have to run**.

| Tool | Where jobs live | Commits with your data | Resumes mid-handler | You also run |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Workhorse** | Your PostgreSQL | Yes, your open transaction | Yes, checkpoints and durable waits | Nothing |
| **pg-boss** | Your PostgreSQL | Yes | No | Nothing |
| **Graphile Worker** | Your PostgreSQL | Yes | No | Nothing |
| **pgmq** | Your PostgreSQL | Yes | No | A PostgreSQL extension |
| **BullMQ** | Redis | No | No | Redis |
| **Temporal** | Its own cluster | No | Yes, a full workflow engine | A Temporal cluster |
| **Inngest** | The provider | No | Yes | A hosted service, or self-hosting |
| **Trigger.dev** | The provider | No | Yes | A hosted service, or self-hosting |

Read it in three bands.

**The PostgreSQL band** is where Workhorse competes directly. pg-boss and Graphile Worker are older
and narrower: both give you transactional enqueue, neither resumes a handler part-way through a
crash. pgmq is a queue primitive rather than a job runner, and it needs an extension, which rules it
out on managed PostgreSQL that will not install one.

**The separate-datastore band** is BullMQ. It is excellent and widely used, but a job in Redis
cannot commit with a row in PostgreSQL, so you are back to reconciling two systems.

**The durable-execution band** is Temporal, Inngest, and Trigger.dev. They do more than Workhorse:
real workflow orchestration, versioning, and their own UIs. They also add a system to your
architecture and put your job state outside your database. Workhorse deliberately trades that power
for having nothing else to operate.

One honest caveat: every tool in that last band has years of production behind it. Workhorse does
not.

If you want a PostgreSQL queue and nothing more, pg-boss and Graphile Worker are mature and
narrower. If you want orchestration as a product, Temporal is the serious answer. Workhorse is for
the case in between: durable execution, in the database you already run, with nothing new to
operate.

## What this release is not

Workhorse is an evidence-first validation release, not a battle-hardened platform. The guarantees
below the surface are tested and documented, but the operational history is short.

Delivery is at least once. A handler can run twice after a crash, and external calls still need
provider idempotency keys. Checkpoints make repeated work safe inside your process; they cannot
make a payment API idempotent for you.
